# EP03 — ศพที่หลอกกองบัญชาการเยอรมัน

**Event:** Operation Mincemeat  
**Period:** April–July 1943  
**Place:** Britain, Huelva (Spain), Mediterranean / Sicily context  
**Target:** 75 scenes x 8s ≈ 10 min, 9:16

## Hook

ชาวประมงสเปนพบศพนายทหารอังกฤษที่มีเอกสารลับติดตัว แต่ตัวตน เอกสาร และภารกิจของเขาถูกสร้างขึ้นเพื่อให้ฝ่ายเยอรมันเชื่อเรื่องที่ไม่เป็นจริง

## 5-act structure

- **Scenes 01–15 — Sicily problem:** Allies prepare for the Mediterranean next move; Sicily is an obvious target, so deception becomes crucial.
- **Scenes 16–30 — Creating Major William Martin:** planners build a believable fictional identity and personal life around a dead body; fake documents point away from Sicily.
- **Scenes 31–45 — Delivery to Spain:** submarine deployment, body released off the Spanish coast, discovery near Huelva, documents enter a chain where German intelligence may gain access.
- **Scenes 46–60 — Did the deception work?:** British intelligence watches for evidence, German command treats the papers as genuine, defensive attention shifts toward Greece/Sardinia/Balkans.
- **Scenes 61–75 — Sicily and legacy:** Operation Husky begins in July 1943; explain what Mincemeat contributed without claiming it alone determined the campaign.

## Principal roles

- Ewen Montagu — British naval intelligence planner.
- Charles Cholmondeley — deception planner.
- “Major William Martin” — fictional identity created for the operation; visual portrayal should distinguish the invented identity from the real deceased person.
- Narrator — primary storyteller.

## Locations

British intelligence offices, document/identity preparation room, submarine interior/deck, Spanish coast near Huelva, offices where papers are handled, Mediterranean maps, Sicily landing context.

## Accuracy notes

- Make clear that “Major William Martin” was a constructed identity.
- Do not invent conversations with Hitler or German intelligence officers.
- Phrase outcome carefully: German forces were redirected/reinforced elsewhere and the deception contributed to preserving surprise for Sicily; it was one component of wider deception.
- Treat the deceased individual respectfully; avoid sensational close-ups.

## Research anchors

Imperial War Museums material on Operation Mincemeat; British official deception records and secondary museum histories.
