# EP08 — สนามเทนนิสที่กลายเป็นแนวรบ

**Event:** Battle of Kohima — fighting around the Deputy Commissioner’s bungalow and tennis court  
**Period:** April–May 1944  
**Place:** Kohima, India  
**Target:** 75 scenes x 8s ≈ 10 min, 9:16

## Hook

สนามเทนนิสเล็ก ๆ บนเนินเขาในอินเดียกลายเป็นแนวหน้าที่ทหารสองฝ่ายอยู่ห่างกันเพียงไม่กี่สิบเมตร และพื้นที่สำหรับกีฬาในยามสงบถูกเปลี่ยนเป็นจุดชี้เป็นชี้ตายของการรบที่ Kohima

## 5-act structure

- **Scenes 01–15 — Why Kohima matters:** Japanese advance toward India, road and supply significance of Kohima, defenders prepare under pressure.
- **Scenes 16–30 — Siege tightens:** garrison positions around the Deputy Commissioner’s bungalow/ridge, shrinking perimeter, supply and water pressure.
- **Scenes 31–45 — The tennis court line:** opposing positions face each other across the court; repeated fighting turns the court and surrounding ground into a symbolic micro-battle.
- **Scenes 46–60 — Reinforcement and counterattack:** British 2nd Division and other forces fight to relieve and recapture positions; 2nd Dorset Regiment’s role culminates in recapture of the tennis court area on 13 May 1944.
- **Scenes 61–75 — Aftermath and meaning:** wider battle continues, Japanese advance is stopped, connect this tiny piece of ground to the larger Imphal-Kohima campaign without claiming the court alone decided the war.

## Principal roles

Use source-verified officers only where necessary; otherwise focus on small groups of British/Indian/Commonwealth and Japanese soldiers. Narrator is primary and must explain geography clearly.

## Locations

Kohima ridge, Deputy Commissioner’s bungalow ruins/grounds, tennis court, trenches and foxholes, supply paths, surrounding jungle hills.

## Accuracy notes

- National Army Museum notes fierce fighting on the tennis court and successful recapture by men of 2nd Battalion, Dorsetshire Regiment on 13 May 1944.
- Avoid reducing the battle to only British versus Japanese forces; acknowledge Indian and other Commonwealth participation in the wider battle.
- Do not create graphic close combat; use distance, exhaustion, terrain and sound to convey intensity.
- Verify exact trench positions and uniform mix before STEP6 image prompts.

## Research anchors

National Army Museum; Kohima Educational Trust; British/Indian campaign histories and museum collections.
