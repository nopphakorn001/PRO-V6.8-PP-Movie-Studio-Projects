# EP02 — ปฏิบัติการโพสต์มาสเตอร์: คืนที่อังกฤษขโมยเรือศัตรูจากท่าเรือของชาติเป็นกลาง

**Event:** Operation Postmaster  
**Date:** 14 January 1942  
**Place:** Santa Isabel, Fernando Po, Spanish Guinea (present-day Malabo, Bioko, Equatorial Guinea)  
**Target:** 75 scenes x 8s ≈ 10 min, 9:16

## Hook

ค่ำคืนในอ่าวของอาณานิคมสเปนที่เป็นกลาง ชายกลุ่มเล็กเตรียมยึดเรือฝ่ายอักษะโดยต้องทำให้ภารกิจดูเหมือนไม่ใช่การโจมตีของรัฐบาลอังกฤษ

## 5-act structure

- **Scenes 01–15 — Why these ships matter:** Atlantic context, neutral Spanish territory, enemy merchant vessels at Santa Isabel, British concern.
- **Scenes 16–30 — Secret planning:** SOE / Small Scale Raiding Force, covert preparation, cover story, route to West Africa, political risk if Spain is angered.
- **Scenes 31–45 — Night of the raid:** approach to harbor, distraction ashore, small teams move toward moored vessels, cutting/taking control without turning the port into a conventional battle.
- **Scenes 46–60 — Ships leave the harbor:** vessels are taken out toward international waters, confusion in port, British naval cover outside the harbor.
- **Scenes 61–75 — Aftermath:** captured ships, diplomatic ambiguity, why the raid remained obscure, what it showed about early SOE raiding methods.

## Principal roles

- Major Gus March-Phillipps — Small Scale Raiding Force commander.
- Geoffrey Appleyard — commando involved in the operation.
- SOE planners / raiding party — use generic supporting identities unless source-verified.
- Spanish colonial officials — background roles; avoid invented political dialogue.
- Narrator — primary storyteller.

## Locations

Santa Isabel harbor, waterfront buildings, moored Axis vessels, Maid Honor / raiding vessel, Atlantic approaches, Lagos/Allied-controlled waters for aftermath.

## Accuracy notes

- Spain was neutral; the political sensitivity is central to the story.
- Avoid presenting every detail of the cover/distraction as certain unless verified from primary or museum sources.
- Do not dramatize the raid as a large firefight; the operation’s unusual character is the covert seizure of vessels.
- Verify ship names, crew counts, exact participating force and post-raid custody before STEP6.

## Research anchors

Imperial War Museums material on Geoffrey Appleyard / Operation Postmaster; SOE histories; British archival sources where available.
