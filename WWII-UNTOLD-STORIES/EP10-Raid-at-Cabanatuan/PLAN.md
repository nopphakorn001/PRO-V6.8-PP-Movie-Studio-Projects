# EP10 — ภารกิจช่วยเชลยกว่า 500 คนหลังแนวข้าศึก

**Event:** Raid at Cabanatuan / The Great Raid  
**Date:** 30 January 1945  
**Place:** Pangatian/Cabanatuan area, Luzon, Philippines  
**Target:** 75 scenes x 8s ≈ 10 min, 9:16

## Hook

ค่ายเชลยอยู่ลึกหลังแนวญี่ปุ่น และผู้ถูกคุมขังจำนวนมากอ่อนแรงจนเดินแทบไม่ได้ ภารกิจจึงไม่ใช่แค่บุกเข้าไป — ทีมช่วยเหลือต้องพาคนกว่า 500 คนออกมาให้ทันก่อนกำลังข้าศึกเข้าถึงพื้นที่

## 5-act structure

- **Scenes 01–15 — Why the prisoners are in danger:** Bataan/POW context kept brief, Cabanatuan camp conditions, Allied advance on Luzon and fear that remaining prisoners may be killed during Japanese withdrawal.
- **Scenes 16–30 — Intelligence and infiltration:** Sixth Army assigns the mission; Alamo Scouts reconnoiter; Rangers move behind enemy lines and link with Filipino guerrillas.
- **Scenes 31–45 — Plan changes:** local intelligence reveals Japanese movement and reinforcements near Cabu Bridge; attack is delayed to improve conditions; guerrillas establish blocking positions.
- **Scenes 46–60 — The raid:** Rangers crawl toward the camp, coordinated assault begins, guards are neutralized, POW barracks are opened, Filipino guerrillas prevent reinforcements from reaching the camp.
- **Scenes 61–75 — Getting everyone home:** weakened prisoners are moved by foot and carabao carts, rescuers withdraw toward friendly lines, medical care and reunion, close on the joint U.S.–Filipino nature of the operation.

## Locations

Rice fields and villages east of friendly lines, guerrilla rendezvous, observation huts/fields around the camp, Cabanatuan/Pangatian POW compound, Cabu Bridge approaches, withdrawal paths, carabao-cart evacuation route.

## Accuracy notes

- Modern U.S. Army commemoration uses 511 Allied POWs rescued; other historical markers/accounts use slightly different totals. Narration should say “กว่า 500 คน” unless citing a specific source/count.
- Make Filipino guerrillas and local civilian support visible; this was not a Rangers-only operation.
- Avoid graphic POW abuse imagery; show malnutrition, fatigue and difficult mobility respectfully.
- Keep chronology clear: reconnaissance and postponement precede the 30 January assault.

## Research anchors

U.S. Army / Army University Press; National WWII Museum oral-history material; Philippine historical marker sources; U.S. Army Special Operations history.
