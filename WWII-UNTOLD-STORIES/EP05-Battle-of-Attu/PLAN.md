# EP05 — สมรภูมิหิมะที่อเมริกาแทบลืม

**Event:** Battle of Attu / Operation Landcrab  
**Date:** 11–30 May 1943  
**Place:** Attu, Aleutian Islands, Alaska  
**Target:** 75 scenes x 8s ≈ 10 min, 9:16

## Hook

ทหารสหรัฐขึ้นฝั่งบนเกาะหนาวจัดที่ญี่ปุ่นยึดไว้ แต่สิ่งที่ทำให้กองกำลังล้มป่วยและหลุดจากแนวรบจำนวนมากไม่ใช่เพียงกระสุน — มันคือความหนาว หมอก โคลน และภูมิประเทศของ Attu เอง

## 5-act structure

- **Scenes 01–15 — Forgotten occupation:** Japanese seizure of Attu/Kiska in 1942, displacement of Attu’s Unangax̂ residents, why the Aleutians mattered.
- **Scenes 16–30 — Landing in fog:** U.S. 7th Infantry Division lands on 11 May 1943 in northern/southern sectors; poor visibility and difficult support.
- **Scenes 31–45 — Weather becomes an enemy:** wet cold, tundra, mountains, supply problems, illness and non-battle casualties; slow advance against concealed Japanese positions.
- **Scenes 46–60 — Closing the pocket:** Americans push toward remaining defenses; commanders change and forces converge; final Japanese counterattack approaches.
- **Scenes 61–75 — Final attack + aftermath:** 29 May mass counterattack, battle ends by the end of the month, casualties and why this U.S. battle remains relatively little known.

## Principal roles

Use a limited composite-level cast for ground soldiers plus source-verified commanders such as Col. Yasuyo Yamasaki and U.S. division leadership when necessary. Narrator remains primary.

## Locations

Massacre Bay, Holtz Bay approaches, mountain passes, tundra ridges, foggy landing beaches, field aid/supply points, Chichagof Harbor area.

## Accuracy notes

- U.S. National Park Service emphasizes that disease and non-battle injuries removed more American soldiers from action than enemy fire; avoid turning this into a simple “snow battle” spectacle.
- Acknowledge the Unangax̂ civilians taken to Japan during the occupation and losses among them.
- Do not depict the final Japanese attack as mindless caricature; describe military circumstances without dehumanizing language.
- Use non-graphic battle imagery.

## Research anchors

U.S. National Park Service; U.S. Army; Naval History and Heritage Command; American Battle Monuments Commission.
